### **Section 5: Technical Interpretation**

The differing values for "Max. DC Input Current" (20+20 A in 2023 vs. 13+13 A in 2024) and "Max. Short Circuit Current" (30+30 A in 2023 vs. 19.5+19.5 A in 2024) are critical. The 2023 data indicates a significantly higher current handling capability than the 2024 data. **If the 2024 specifications are accurate, connecting solar panels that can produce currents exceeding 13A per string (or 19.5A short-circuit) to this inverter would pose a serious safety risk, potentially leading to equipment damage, fire, or electric shock.** This difference of 7A per string in maximum operating current and 10.5A per string in short-circuit current is substantial and could have severe implications for system design, safety, and warranty.

The IP65 Ingress Protection rating (consistent across both sources) indicates that the inverter is protected against dust ingress and low-pressure water jets from any direction. This means it is suitable for outdoor installation where it may be exposed to rain and dust, but not for submersion or high-pressure water.

The conflicting terminology for "Max. Active Power" (2023) vs. "Max. AC Output Apparent Power" (2024) is also important. Active power (kW) is the real power consumed by a load, while apparent power (kVA) is the total power flowing in an AC circuit. While numerically similar in this instance (5.5 kW vs 5.5 kVA), it's crucial to confirm which rating is the actual limit for sustained operation and grid export, as kVA includes reactive power and is typically higher than or equal to kW.

The discrepancies in efficiency ratings (Max. Efficiency and Euro Efficiency) suggest either product revisions or different testing methodologies between the two data sheets. While these might not be safety-critical, they affect performance expectations and financial projections for energy yield. Similarly, the differing cooling concepts ("Free Cooling, Smart Cooling" vs. "Natural Cooling") could impact the inverter's thermal management and long-term reliability in varying ambient temperatures.
